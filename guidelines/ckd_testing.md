# CKD testing (my summary, sources: KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline; ADA Standards of Care)
Yearly testing in diabetes: adults with type 2 diabetes should have both a blood test (eGFR) and a urine test (UACR) at least once a year to check for chronic kidney disease.
Testing in high blood pressure: adults with high blood pressure are at high risk for CKD and should be tested with eGFR and UACR.
Definition of CKD: CKD is present when eGFR is below 60 or UACR is 30 mg/g or higher for more than 3 months.
Confirming one abnormal result: a single abnormal eGFR or UACR result should be repeated within 3 months to confirm CKD.
Why the urine test matters: UACR can detect kidney damage early, even when eGFR is still normal.
